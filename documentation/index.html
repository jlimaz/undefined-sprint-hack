Argus
RF intelligence.

Air-gapped by design. A local classifier reads the signal, and a local LLM explains it.

The problem, in DIU’s words

Raw RF is easy to capture.
Insight takes deep expertise.

The ask: pair SDR output with LLMs or multimodal AI to

  • IDENTIFYCharacterize signal metadata: frequency, bandwidth, modulation
  • EXPLAINTranslate complex spectral visualizations into plain-language summaries
  • FLAGFlag anomalies in the ambient spectrum
  • OPENRely only on open-source, publicly documented tools and protocols
The scenario

Signals everywhere. No one to read them.

A Ukrainian on the front line, with no technical background and no experience with military equipment, needs to understand the RF around him.

He can capture itA commercial SDR records the spectrum around his position.
He cannot read itFrequencies, bandwidths and modulations mean little without RF training.
He needs to knowWhat is transmitting nearby, and whether it is a threat, in plain words.

Why Laya

The expertise is text, not code.

Conditional logic
  • Thresholds collide where signal types overlap in bandwidth.
  • Every new signal type means new code.
  • No confidence, so nothing to flag.
Classic ML classifier
  • Labels are frozen at training time.
  • A new question means a new model.
  • Nearest-neighbour reference: 82.7%.
Laya
  • Reads the question and its answers as text, at request time.
  • An RF expert edits a JSON file, not the code.
  • Calibrated confidence, and no text to hallucinate.
  • Fine-tuned: 91.6%.

Laya is an open-source 421M-parameter encoder: one forward pass, deterministic, Apache-2.0. Accuracy is on 774 held-out rows.

Fine-tuning

How Laya learned RF.

A first attempt, 12 emitter families on simulated data, stalled at 21%. We rebuilt the task around real recordings.

01
Data
Panoradio HF recordings for eight shortwave signal types, plus the sigidwiki catalogue for “other”.
→
02
Task
One question with nine answers. Each observation is written as a sentence Laya can read.
→
03
Train
Laya’s official script on the base checkpoint: about 12,000 items in about 8 hours.
→
04
Test
774 rows held out. “Other” is tested on 29 signal types never seen in training.

Accuracy on the held-out rows 91.6% Nearest-neighbour reference 82.7%

Limits and potential

Limits today. Room to grow.

Limits
  • Data. Little field-specific RF data to train on.
  • Time. One sprint for fine-tuning and integration.
  • Hardware. Small GPUs slow training and inference.
Potential
  • Field data. More domain data to fine-tune Laya.
  • Hardware. Better machines for the inference models.
  • Personalization. Easy to adapt: signal types and questions are plain text.
